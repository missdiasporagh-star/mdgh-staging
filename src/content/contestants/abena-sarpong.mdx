She learned to weave from her grandfather before she learned to write — and noticed, sometime in her twenties, that the patterns she grew up watching him make weren't in any of the books her university used to teach textile history.

Heritage in Hands is the project that came out of that gap: a paid archive, run by the families who hold the patterns, distributed back to the schools that taught around them.
